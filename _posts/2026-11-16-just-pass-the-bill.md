What if _when_ a legislation is passed doesn't matter?

I'm not currently convinced that voters assign the benefits of specific bills to who passed them. Sure, sometimes a moniker like "Obamacare" sticks. But the idea that exactly when [energy infrastructure bills get passed](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/12/opinion/democrats-congress-affordability-reform.html) matters to voters would require, frankly, that the country's average memory remembers (1) who exactly passed a bill, (2) what that bill was supposed to do, and (3) how that bill impacts them today.

Trump's repeated ping-ponging of elected office to me suggests that our ability to remember what elected official actually did and how their past actions impact today or tomorrow is piss-poor. Healthcare is one thing: it's tangible, we see it every day. Energy infrastructure that will impact our electricity bill in 10-20 years? No one will remember who passed it exactly and not everyone will believe it actually lowered energy prices There's plenty of time to clamor for credit.
